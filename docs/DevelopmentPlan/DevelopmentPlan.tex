\section*{Appendix --- Team Charter}

\subsection*{External Goals}

\subsubsection{Capstone EXPO Performance}
The team aims to win a prize at the Capstone EXPO, but more importantly, provide an intriguing and interesting demo and presentation that attendees will find memorable.

\subsubsection{Capstone course Performance}
The team aims to receive a high mark (A+) in the Capstone course to reflect the effort that will go into the project.

\subsubsection{Learning}
Each member of the capstone team has expressed that a top priority for the course is learning new technical skills and technologies through the project. This spans to beyond technical skills, for qualities
such as leadership, teamwork, and public speaking.

\subsubsection{Have Fun}
This course is one of the final opportunities for the team to work together and create an impressive final product. The journey should be a fun challenge for the team.

\subsection*{Attendance}

\subsubsection*{Expectations}

The team expects all members to attend a minimum of 80\% of weekly scheduled team meetings. This does not apply to supplementary ad-hoc meetings. Leaving early is admissible if the member has another commitment (academic or extra-curricular). Missing meetings is admissible as long as the member provides a reasonable excuse to the team as early as possible.
This policy for missing meetings applies to a given limit. If a team member misses 2 consecutive meetings, a mediator from the team will reach out to the member in question to try and resolve the attendance issue and determine the best next step.

\subsubsection*{Acceptable Excuse}

An acceptable excuse is when external factors are involved (outside of usual academic and extra-curricular deadlines for other commitments). External factors would be considered family commitments, medical, or other serious issues.

Unacceptable excuses are those without reason or those involving other course deadlines. As upper year students it is expected for us to manage many deadlines across multiple courses and other course deadlines are not a typically acceptable reason to be late on capstone work.
In the case that work is projected to finish late, ensure other team members are aware so that they may step in to assist if available.

\subsubsection*{In Case of Emergency}

Team members will try their best to inform other team members of their absence. The team also acknowledges that this is not always possible in an emergency situation.

\subsection*{Accountability and Teamwork}

\subsubsection*{Quality} 

It is expected that all team members stay up to date on project happenings and the agenda for an upcoming meeting. Team members are considered responsible for bringing themselves up to speed on current decisions and actions so that they can contribute effectively.

In terms of deliverables, it is expected that documents are reviewed for clarity, grammar, and spelling. In addition, the document should be in alignment with level 4 of the corresponding rubric to maximize the team's mark.
For code deliverables, it is expected that the code will pass the CI quality gate as well as all test cases (including the test cases that have been added for the new feature).

\subsubsection*{Attitude}

The expectation for attitude is as follows: maintaining a co-operative, kind, and open attitude will result in the best final product. During meetings, it's important not only to arrive prepared, but also
be an effective participant and listener who acts respectfully at all times.

With regards to feedback, always approach it from the philosophy that constructive feedback is beneficial in the long run and should not be taken personally. Those who are giving feedback should not be unnecessarily
critical or attacking and should instead focus on areas for improvement.

\subsubsection{Conflict \& Feedback Resolution Plan}

In the case of conflict, a team member uninvolved will act as a mediator between the two parties. 
In addition to both parties turning away from aggression, hostility, and negative comments, the mediator 
must ensure that both parties are adhering to this nature in an unbiased manner and the mediator must show empathy to both parties. 
The mediator may also decide to schedule a follow up to ensure all parties are able to continue discussing a conflict in a healthy 
manner or to ensure that it has been resolved.

\subsubsection*{Stay on Track}

The primary method used to keep the team on track will be GitHub issues. GitHub issues allow the team to track a variety of deadlines and components of the project, including meetings, code tasks, and lectures.
By assigning team members to issues within the GitHub project, the team is able to guarantee a roughly equal split of work. Due to the variable nature of the project and unknown targets for total code and commits, no target metrics will be set for commits at this point. Instead, we aim to measure participation via the code review process (comments and recommendations on pull requests). This way,
the team can ensure that members are staying up to date with code changes they did not write, while also contributing to improving the quality of the overall product. Each pull request should have a minimum of two reviewers, and the split of total reviews should be roughly 20\% per team member.

As stated earlier in the team contract, the team is setting a target metric of 80\% attendance to all team meetings. 

In the case that a team member does not meet their target metrics or not contribute their fair share and the reason is unknown or unacceptable, the team will host a meeting to find the root cause of the imbalance. In the case that further accommodations are required (e.g. a team member is facing a significant challenge outside of school), the issue will be
raised upwards to be handled appropriately. Otherwise, the team will try to reach a consensus. For example, in order to try and even out the share of work, the team member in question may take on a greater share of the next deliverables and tasks to balance out workload in the long run. Because the team members have been friends
for years, the team aims to solve any imbalances in a cordial fashion while keeping in mind that the work split at a specific time may not be equal as commitments vary. The expecation, however, is that in the long term the contributions average out and become roughly equal.

There is currently no specific reward set for team members who go above and beyond. A Potential reward is the team member not having to contribute to their share of the next team social event as a ''thank you'' from the rest of the team.

\subsubsection*{Team Building}

The team will aim to spend time together that is not focused on capstone at a minimum of once per month. The team began this tradition in September by meeting for a dinner on the first weekend of the semester.
Suggestions for further team building events include an escape room, board games, or seeing a game of one of the two new professional hockey teams in Hamilton.

\subsubsection*{Decision Making} 

Whenever the group is faced with a decision, a consensus strategy will be used. The consensus strategy allows each group member to share their opinion. 
In the ideal case, all team members are able to arrive at the same conclusion or compromise if necessary. In the case a consensus is not possible, a voting system will be implemented 
where the majority vote will be the result of the decision. It should be noted that the voting system is intended to act as a last resort when attempts at reaching 
consensus through healthy discussion have been exhausted.
